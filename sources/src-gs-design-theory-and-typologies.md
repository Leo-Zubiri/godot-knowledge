- **MDA (AAAI listing, abstract read):** Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, published 20 June 2004 in the AAAI Workshop Papers 2004; MDA = Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics; developed and taught in the Game Design and Tuning Workshop at GDC (San Jose) 2001–2004; presented as a structured way to analyse games, bridging game design/development, game criticism and technical game research. The PDF text was not extractable; the eight "aesthetics" (sensation, fantasy, narrative, challenge, fellowship, discovery, expression, submission) are from search descriptions only (`unverified`). No empirical validation found; it works as vocabulary.
- **Bartle, "Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades: Players Who Suit MUDs" (1996, read):** derived from an extended online debate (Nov 1989–May 1990) among ~15 core and ~15 occasional senior players of one commercial UK MUD with player-killing, summarised by the author as senior administrator. Four types — achievers (goals, points), explorers (understanding the world), socialisers (relationships), killers (affecting other players) — mapped on acting vs interacting and world vs players. **Author's own caveats:** not a trained psychologist, no conventionally rigorous method, types are stereotypes not individuals, model imprecise, ideal mix would need per-MUD questionnaires he did not run, role-playing MUDs not studied. Qualitative balance claims (not measured): more killers drive off achievers and hurt socialisers most; explorers largely unaffected; socialisers volatile; a balanced mix is hardest but most stable.
- **Hamari and Tuunanen, "Player Types: A Meta-synthesis" (ToDiGRA 1(2), 10 Mar 2014, DOI 10.26503/todigra.v1i2.13, abstract read):** literature synthesis of earlier typologies; seven dimensions — Intensity, Achievement, Exploration, Sociability, Domination, Immersion, In-game demographics. The visible page shows **no** explicit warnings about typologies (an earlier agent claim of a "self-fulfilling" warning is not confirmed by this page).
