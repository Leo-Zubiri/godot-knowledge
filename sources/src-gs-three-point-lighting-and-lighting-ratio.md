## Three-point lighting
- **Key light:** the subject's "principal illuminator"; "more than anything else, the strength, color and angle of the key determines the shot's overall lighting design". Typically 30–45 degrees to one side of the camera–subject axis and raised 30–45 degrees.
- **Fill light:** lights the shaded areas left by the key, usually from the opposite side at about camera height; brightness "often up to half that of the key"; a reflector (white card, white wall) can act as fill. "A fill light that is too intense gives a flat, low-contrast image." No fill gives stark contrast (low-key lighting).
- **Backlight:** behind and usually above the subject; provides "a rim of light, which separates the subject from the background" and picks out contours.
- **Fourth (background) light:** lights walls or scenery behind the subject; adds depth.

## Lighting ratio
- The comparison of key to fill: "The higher the lighting ratio, the higher the contrast of the image."
- American Society of Cinematographers definition: (key + fill) : fill, with all fill lights summed. Examples: key 200 fc and fill 100 fc give 3:1; key 800 and fill 200 give 5:1.
- Each stop doubles the light: 0 stops = 1:1, 2 stops = 4:1, 3 stops = 8:1. A key one stop brighter than the fill is 2:1 measured source-to-source but 3:1 by the additive method.
