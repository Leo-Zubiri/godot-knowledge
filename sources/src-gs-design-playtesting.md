- **Nielsen, "Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users" (NN/g, 18 Mar 2000, read):** small tests are the most efficient way to find usability problems; the number found follows diminishing returns, modelled as N·(1−(1−L)^n) with L≈31% on average across his projects (his figure); three rounds of five beat one round of fifteen because you fix and retest; with two distinct user groups test 3–4 from each, with three or more at least 3 each; quantitative usability metrics need about 20 users and card sorting about 15; the model assumes comparable users and the 2000 article points to newer analysis. **It is a usability-problem model for software, not a measure of fun or balance** (our reading); transferring "5 users" to game fun or telemetry is a weak inference.
- **Valve, "Valve's Approach to Playtesting" (GDC 2009 PDF) — unreadable (binary), claims unverified (search summary only):** direct observation matters more than what players say; an observer's presence can bias results. Valve's GDC 2006 "Half-Life 2 Design Process" and a Valve developer's Portal article are likewise known only from search summaries.
