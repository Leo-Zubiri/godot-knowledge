- **Game Accessibility Guidelines, basic tier (read):** grouped by need. *Motor:* adjustable game speed/sensitivity/haptics (or haptics off), large well-spaced on-screen buttons, same input method for menus as gameplay, simple or alternative controls, remappable controls. *Cognitive:* avoid flashing images and repetitive patterns, let players advance text at their own pace, interactive tutorials and plain language, start playing without deep menus, simple text formatting and readable default font size. *Vision:* strong contrast between text/UI and background, avoid VR motion-sickness triggers, sensible default FOV, **do not rely on colour alone** for essential information. *Hearing:* clear subtitles/captions, no essential information through sound alone, separate volume controls/mutes for effects, speech and music, subtitles for all important speech. *Speech:* never require voice input. *General:* ask for accessibility feedback, save and remember all settings, describe accessibility features in-game and on packaging/website, wide range of difficulty levels. **The page states no numeric thresholds** (no contrast ratios, minimum font sizes or button sizes); it notes remapping, text size, colourblindness and subtitle presentation as the most commonly complained-about issues.
- **Xbox Accessibility Guidelines (Microsoft Learn, read):** version 3.2 published 8 Jun 2023 (page updated Aug 2026); best practices developed with industry experts and members of the Gaming & Disability Community; for designers (idea catalyst), developers (guardrails) and test teams (validation checklist); **explicitly not a checklist for compliance or legal requirements**. Each guideline has goal, overview, scoping questions, key areas, implementation guidelines, examples, player impact and resources. (Specific guideline numbers such as 101 text display or 102 contrast were seen only in search summaries on this fetch; the intro page names them via links.)
- **EBU R 128 (tech.ebu.ch, read):** programme loudness target **−23 LUFS**; deviation limited to **±0.5 LU** since version 3.0 (2014); also uses loudness range and maximum true peak level (numeric limits not on the page); current version 5.0, November 2023. It is a broadcast recommendation, not a game-specific standard.
- **Pointer, unread (search summary, `unverified`):** ITU-R BS.2434 (2018) reportedly cites typical game loudness around −24±2 LKFS on consoles and −18±2 LKFS on portable devices, and regional targets (Japan −24±1, North America −24±2 per A/85, Europe −23±0.5 LUFS). No source confirmed for platform-specific targets (Sony, Apple).
