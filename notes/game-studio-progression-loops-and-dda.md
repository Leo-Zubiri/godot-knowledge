> **Evidence status:** primary papers/pages as read on 2026-11-08 (paraphrased; PDFs read from page images, partly). Practitioner theory is labelled as such. Status `proposed`.

- **Loops and arcs (Cook, 2012; practitioner theory):** a loop = repeated action, feedback, model update; an arc = mostly pre-authored payload you do not repeat. Useful as a vocabulary test (what repeats?); no data behind it. "Chemistry of Game Design" (2007) is belief, disputed in its own comments; skill-atom definition UNV.
- **Reward schedules:** claims that variable-ratio schedules maximise response rate come from search summaries (UNV); transfer to games and ethics are contested.
- **Hamlet DDA (Hunicke & Chapman):** monitors game statistics, predicts inventory shortfall ("flailing") with a Gaussian model under stated assumptions, and intervenes with *reactive* (on-stage) or *proactive* (off-stage) actions under *policies* (Comfort Zone ~50% health; Discomfort Zone 15-20%). The pages read use future tense for unfinished parts and contain **no player evaluation**: it does not demonstrate that DDA improves outcomes (pp. 1-5 read; conclusions not).
- **Balance books/blogs and scope postmortems:** pointers only (Game Balance, Machinations, postmortem claims untraced). Interpretation: treat self-reported postmortems as biased toward survivors.
