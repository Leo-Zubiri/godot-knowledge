> **Evidence status:** primary paper read in full on 2026-11-08 from an unofficial copy (paraphrased; see the cited source). Status `proposed`.

- **Derivation:** 285 usability problems coded from 108 GameSpot PC reviews across 6 genres, grouped into 12 categories, turned into ten heuristics (the authors' own method; reviews are a secondary, editor-written record).
- **Heuristics (short):** consistent responses; customizable settings/difficulty/speed; predictable computer-controlled units; unobstructed, appropriate views; skippable non-playable content; intuitive and remappable input; controllable sensitivity; visible game status; instructions, training and help; readable visual representations.
- **Evidence strength:** the only test was five evaluators on one PC demo (45 problems); the authors call it preliminary. Treat the list as a structured checklist for inspections, not as validated predictors of player outcomes (interpretation).
- **Scope limits stated by the authors:** usability only (not fun, engagement or story); console, multiplayer and serious games not studied.
- **Use here (interpretation):** a cheap pre-playtest review layer for prototypes, and several items (remappable input, skippable content, adjustable difficulty) double as accessibility minimums.
